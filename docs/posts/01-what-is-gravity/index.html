What Is Gravity, Really?

Before we can talk about orbits, we need to understand the force that makes them. Meet the inverse-square law.

January 6, 2025 #gravity #basics #newton

Welcome to Orbital Notes. Over this short series we’ll build up — from absolute scratch — an understanding of how spacecraft, moons, and planets move through space. No prior physics needed. We start at the very beginning: the force behind everything that follows.

Gravity is just attraction

Every object with mass pulls on every other object with mass. You pull on the Earth, and the Earth pulls on you. The reason you notice the Earth’s pull (and not the other way around) is that the Earth is enormously more massive.

Isaac Newton wrote this down in 1687 as the law of universal gravitation:

F=G m1m2r2F = G\,\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}F=Gr2m1​m2​​

Let’s decode every symbol, because this one equation powers the entire series:

  • FFF — the gravitational force between the two objects.
  • m1m_1m1​ and m2m_2m2​ — the two masses (say, a planet and a satellite).
  • rrr — the distance between their centres.
  • GGG — the gravitational constant, a tiny fixed number, G≈6.674×10−11G \approx 6.674 \times 10^{-11}G≈6.674×10−11, that sets the overall strength of gravity.

The most important part: that little r2r^2r2

Notice the distance rrr is squared and sits on the bottom of the fraction. That single detail shapes everything in space. It means gravity follows an inverse-square law: double your distance and the force doesn’t halve — it drops to a quarter. Triple it, and you’re down to a ninth.

See how steeply the curve falls near the surface and then flattens out? Gravity never quite reaches zero — it just gets weaker and weaker forever. That “weak but never gone” tail is exactly what lets distant moons stay bound to their planets.

Why this matters for orbits

Here’s the key idea to carry forward: gravity is always pulling you toward the centre of whatever massive body you’re near. It never pushes. So if everything is being pulled down, how does anything stay up in space?

That puzzle — how to fall without ever hitting the ground — is the secret of orbits, and it’s exactly where we go next.
