A **cache** stores the results of expensive work close to where it is needed so
repeated requests are served cheaply. The hard parts are placement, eviction and
invalidation.

## Effective latency

If a cache serves a fraction $h$ of requests (the **hit ratio**) at latency
$t_\text{hit}$ and the rest fall through to the origin at $t_\text{miss}$, the
average latency is:

$$
t_\text{avg} = h \cdot t_\text{hit} + (1 - h) \cdot t_\text{miss}
$$

Because $t_\text{miss} \gg t_\text{hit}$, even modest hit ratios pay off quickly —
and the returns are steep as $h$ approaches 1:

## Invalidation

> There are only two hard things in computer science: cache invalidation and
> naming things.

Common strategies include **time-to-live (TTL)** expiry, **write-through** caches
that update on every write, and **cache-aside**, where the application loads and
populates the cache on a miss.
